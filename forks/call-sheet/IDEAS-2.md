# Call Sheet ideas, round 2 — from football history

November 17, 2026. Built from real football history (eras, schemes, special teams, traditions), not from other
games. Each idea says which existing system it reuses. **Status (Nov 17): built.** Remaining simplifications (see HANDOFF-GPT.md): #7 two-way players are the Two-Way Star
tactic and Two-Way era; no player fills roles on both sides, because Dynasty Lab's depth chart puts each player at one
position. #24 team colors are on pack card backs, not every card. Closed since: quick kick (#5), drill timeouts that
stop the clock (#13), the trophy case (#18), walk-on breakouts (#21), and spring game recommendations through camp (#22).

## Special teams become cards

1. **Kicking duels.** Field goals and punts are decided by the kicker's real power/technique against a block or
   return duel, instead of a flat chance. Cards: *Rugby Punt*, *Pooch Kick*, *Squib*, *Hands Team* (onside recovery).
   Reuses the 4th-down choice and the kicker/punter cards that now exist.
2. **Fakes.** *Fake Punt* and *Fake Field Goal* (a holder pass or run) as once-per-game trick cards on 4th down, with
   a staff-recommended risk line. Coaching history is full of famous fakes; this makes 4th down a card decision.
3. **The onside kick.** A late-game card when trailing: recovery chance from the hands team's real players.
4. **Swinging gate.** An old two-point / PAT formation as a trick card: shift everyone wide, snap to the up-back.
5. **Quick kick.** A pre-1970s staple: punt on 3rd down from a running formation to flip the field. A pure field
   position card that the era system could favor.

## Eras of football as seasons and sets

6. **Single-wing and leather-helmet era cards.** *Spinner Series*, *Buck Lateral*, *Single-Wing Power*: run-heavy
   cards with direct snaps. A "Single Wing" era season favors them (the existing era system).
7. **Two-way players.** In the early-era set, a player's card shows offense and defense, and one card occupies a
   role on both sides. Rewards versatile athletes (the `versatility` trait finally matters everywhere).
8. **Veer, wishbone and flexbone set.** Triple option as a three-duel read (dive, keep, pitch), each decided by a
   different defender. A natural album and a coaching-tree card for option coordinators.
9. **West Coast and run-and-shoot set.** Timing routes, option routes, choice reads. Completes the passing album.
10. **The 46 and the zone blitz.** Defensive era cards: *46 Bear*, *Zone Blitz* (a lineman drops into coverage),
    *Fire Zone Exchange*. A Blitz Era season makes them sing.

## Battles

11. **Personnel groupings.** Choose 11, 12, 21 or 10 personnel before a series: more tight ends help the run,
    more receivers help the pass. The defense answers with base, nickel or dime. Reuses the backfield-choice
    mechanic and real depth.
12. **Audibles at the line.** A tactic that swaps your play after the defense shows its look, at a Command cost.
    Information already exists (Hard Count, Check-With-Me); this adds the classic "kill, kill" call.
13. **Clock management.** Timeouts become a real resource (three per half, already tracked by the engine), not
    just cards; the drill uses them.
14. **Red zone package.** Inside the 20, the field shrinks: deep shots lose value, fades and power runs gain.
    A situational card set and a labeled edge like the drill.
15. **Weather that changes during the game.** The real forecast already drives conditions; let rain arrive in the
    second half, so a weather deck pays off.

## Dynasty

16. **Bowl traditions.** Each bowl has a pack theme (an old-school bowl gives era cards, a newer one gives modern
    cards). Reuses the stage packs.
17. **Heisman watch as a card chase.** Your best player's signature card upgrades as his award case grows
    (Player of the Week → All-Conference → Heisman finalist). Reuses awards and signatures.
18. **Rivalry trophy cards.** The trophy itself is a card that sits in your collection while you hold it, with a
    small labeled edge in that rivalry game only. Reuses rivalry week and rival-watch history.
19. **Coaching tree lineage.** When an assistant leaves to become a head coach elsewhere, his card leaves with him,
    and that program's playbook starts using it against you. Reuses staff departures and AI playbooks.
20. **Program history sets.** Albums for your own program: "First bowl win", "Undefeated season", "National
    title", each completed by real events in your dynasty's history.
21. **Walk-on to starter.** Low-star players who earn a starting role get a "Walk-On" card frame and a one-time
    breakout chance, celebrating development over recruiting rank.
22. **Spring game.** An offseason exhibition card battle between your own first and second team, with real
    depth-chart consequences (a camp audition at full scale).

## Collecting and presentation

23. **Card grades.** Pulls have a print quality (base, foil, gold) that is purely cosmetic but makes packs exciting
    without adding power.
24. **Program-branded card backs.** Your team colors and helmet on every card back; opponents' cards in theirs.
25. **Career cards.** When a player graduates, his card gets a stat line on the back from his real career and goes
    into the Ring of Honor binder.
